## Overview

As-built outline for the Thesis 1 proposal defense deck (`SynapseOS_Proposal_Defense.pptx`, generated by `build-deck.js`) — Chapters 1-3 of SynapseOS, defending the research plan and methodology rather than completed results, since the user study has not run yet (IRB still pending per `docs/scope.md` Track B). Content is pulled from the consolidated proposal (`../consolidated/SynapseOS_Proposal_Chapters_1_to_3.html`), the held-out round-7 results (`prototype/pilot/results_round7.txt`), and the project's own decision log (`docs/decisions.md`), not invented for the deck. Two things it deliberately builds in: the round-7 numbers are reported as a frozen held-out result with its own limits named on the slide (single annotator, `open-problems.md` row 27), never oversold as final study findings, and the one open question the adviser has already asked once in writing (the composition/resolution ablation, `open-problems.md` row 38) gets its own slide instead of being left for the panel to raise cold. Five revisions since the first build: text sized for reading from the back of a defense room; the dual-metric chart and the scope+status slide were each split in two; notes settled at 2-3 short, plain-language sentences after swinging too thin and then too dense; every quote-bearing slide's note now opens by pointing at what's on screen before explaining it; Linux was removed from the narration (it's this prototype's test substrate, not the thesis's claim — D27/D28 make the same distinction in the proposal itself); and a closing "What's Next" slide was added before Q&A, framed generically rather than naming specific course numbers.

## Open items before presenting

- **Slide 6 placeholder → a looping GIF.** A short, auto-playing, looping recording of one full exchange (request → generated command → confirmation gate → result) is the stronger choice here over a static image: it's the "this is real and working" moment of the deck, and motion holds attention while you narrate over it without needing to click anything. PowerPoint autoplays and loops an embedded animated GIF during the slideshow on its own. Produce it with the `css171/tools/record-demo.sh`-derived pipeline (Xvfb + xterm + ffmpeg, `RECORD_PREVIEW=1` already gives a cropped `preview.gif`) on the disposable VM — see `../../docs/testing-environment.md` — then swap it in with `slide.addImage({ path: "demo.gif", x, y, w, h })` in `build-deck.js` in place of the `terminalPlaceholder()` call.
- **Slide 8 placeholder → a static screenshot.** This slide is making one precise technical point — the safety gate actually stopping a destructive command — and a still image is easier to read and reason about while you're explaining it than looping motion would be competing for attention. Crop tightly to the confirmation prompt itself.
- Round-7 figures on slides 10-11 are current as of this build (`prototype/pilot/results_round7.txt`); re-pull them if the analyser or corpus changes before the defense date.
- Notes are short, plain-language cues (2-3 sentences each) meant to be paraphrased in your own delivery, not read verbatim word-for-word — they trade some technical precision for being quick to say and easy for a non-specialist listener to follow; the precise wording lives in the thesis chapters themselves.
