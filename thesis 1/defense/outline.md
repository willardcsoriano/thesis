## Overview

As-built outline for the Thesis 1 proposal defense deck (`SynapseOS_Proposal_Defense.pptx`, generated by `build-deck.js`) — Chapters 1-3 of SynapseOS, defending the research plan and methodology rather than completed results, since the user study has not run yet (IRB still pending per `docs/scope.md` Track B). Content is pulled from the consolidated proposal (`../consolidated/SynapseOS_Proposal_Chapters_1_to_3.html`), the held-out round-7 results (`prototype/pilot/results_round7.txt`), and the project's own decision log (`docs/decisions.md`), not invented for the deck. Two things it deliberately builds in: the round-7 numbers are reported as a frozen held-out result with its own limits named on the slide (single annotator, `open-problems.md` row 27), never oversold as final study findings, and the one open question the adviser has already asked once in writing (the composition/resolution ablation, `open-problems.md` row 38) gets its own slide instead of being left for the panel to raise cold. Started as a 16-item draft reviewed before building; two pairs of slides merged during the build (research questions + objectives; scope/sequencing + current status) and one new slide was added (a real-exchange visual) — this file reflects what actually got built, not the original pitch.

## Slide outline

1. **Title** — SynapseOS: Designing a Conversational Interface Layer for Personal Computing. Team, department.
2. **The gap** — command line vs. GUI exclusion; Berkeley UNIX Consultant (1988) → LLMs reopen the thread; no implemented, evaluated system covers the whole Linux desktop session.
3. **Problem statement** — direct quote from Ch.1.
4. **Research questions** — RQ1 (algorithm) first, RQ2-4 (interface) following, with a callout that this ordering means two independent contributions, not one subordinate to the other (D32). Objectives folded in via speaker notes rather than a separate slide.
5. **Conceptual framework** — the four-layer pipeline (Input → Reasoning → Action → OS Integration) as a built diagram, not a screenshot of Figure 3.1.
6. **What it looks like** — *placeholder.* A real conversational exchange (request → command → confirmation gate → result) goes here once captured and verified.
7. **The algorithm (RQ1) — the problem** — pattern lists judge a command by name, not effect.
8. **The algorithm (RQ1) — what it claims** — the two abilities only, per D34: composition through wrappers, resolution of run-time targets; explicitly narrow, unknown fails closed. *Placeholder* for a real capture of the safety gate catching a destructive command.
9. **Evaluation design — two independent studies** — corpus study (no participants, answers RQ1) vs. user study (40 participants, answers RQ2-4), side by side.
10. **Algorithm result — held-out corpus (777 commands, round 7)** — two charts, real data: silent data loss (pattern list 41.2%, fail-closed list and algorithm both 0.0%) and restoration coverage of the 325 executable losses (pattern list 41.5% unverified, fail-closed list 0.0%, algorithm 89.5%). Caption names the single-annotator limit.
11. **Interface evaluation plan (RQ2-4)** — within-subjects design, two populations, task suite, measures.
12. **Scope, sequencing & current status** — direct response to "manage scope carefully" (D36's priority order), plus a three-row status table: algorithm essentially done, build in progress, ethics/IRB the named bottleneck.
13. **One question we want to raise ourselves** — the composition-ablation question (row 38), stated as a quote, with the current prose answer and the concrete plan to close it.
14. **Expected contribution** — three cards, one per RQ grouping.
15. **Q&A** — closing slide.

Timing at ~1.5-2 min/slide: 14 content slides ≈ 21-28 minutes, leaving room for questions.
